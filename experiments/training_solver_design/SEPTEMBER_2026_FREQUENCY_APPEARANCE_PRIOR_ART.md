## Laplacian Frequency Hierarchies (LFH)

Source: [paper v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.03334v1), especially §§3.3, 4.1–4.2, 5 and Appendices A/G; [author project](https://sorenzhang574.github.io/Laplacian-GS/); [author code](https://github.com/SorenZhang574/Laplacian-GS). LFH trains separate frequency fields, archives learned coarse fields, and trains a finer field with image-domain composition. Default is two levels at 10K/20K iterations, with a resolution pyramid. Thus simply freezing an easy/low-frequency field and training a smaller residual field is occupied prior art. Its staged images/loss are not our every-step original-full-resolution protocol.

Author Table 1 (1K setting, NVIDIA L40, 30K, Mip-NeRF 360 average) reports FastGS-Big 4.82 min / 27.96 PSNR / .820 SSIM / .216 LPIPS versus LFH(FastGS) 3.96 min / 27.67 / .809 / .233. Deep Blending: 3.34 / 30.24 / .911 / .240 versus 3.04 / 30.16 / .907 / .257. Tanks & Temples: 3.44 / 24.34 / .858 / .173 versus 3.13 / 24.03 / .842 / .205. Thus all three quality metrics regress in all three aggregate comparisons despite shorter reported time. Tables 2–3 likewise report 2K and 4K Mip-NeRF 360 aggregates with faster LFH(FastGS) but worse three-metric quality: at 4K, 8.74 / 27.37 / .812 / .327 versus 6.57 / 27.22 / .807 / .337. These are author results, not our A100 comparisons.

Appendix G says main time includes iterative optimization, excludes pyramid preprocessing and archive disk I/O; two archive writes took 16.72 s in the profiled garden run. It attributes 56.5% of LFH(FastGS) iterative time to backward on that run. The paper's 1K hardware is L40 and 2K/4K hardware is Pro6000. Neither its time nor our A100 profile is a common-protocol complete-wall comparison.

The pinned author [README](https://github.com/SorenZhang574/Laplacian-GS/blob/acfc5602be0063fba5fc6cd70235f95efd2b0091/README.md) lines 22/63 says this release includes Mip-NeRF 360 **1K** reproduction scripts; it does not yet provide a 2K/4K reproduction script. The [1K FastGS script](https://github.com/SorenZhang574/Laplacian-GS/blob/acfc5602be0063fba5fc6cd70235f95efd2b0091/run_mipnerf360_1k_fastgs.sh) line 60 uses `--grad_abs_thresh 0.0003` for room, whereas our pinned FastGS-Big room protocol uses `0.0004`. This is a concrete configuration mismatch to resolve before any direct local comparison, not evidence that the paper's author results are invalid.

## Compact Neural Appearance Models

Source: [paper v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05255v1), §§4–6 and Appendices B/C; [author project](https://fhahlbohm.github.io/efficient-gaussian-appearance); [author code](https://github.com/nerficg-project/efficient-gaussian-appearance). The paper replaces per-Gaussian SH appearance with a compact latent and shared tiny MLP, fusing forward and backward decoding into a differentiable CUDA rasterizer. This occupies the direct “compressed shared appearance improves geometry while reducing parameter traffic” core. Its baseline is **Faster-GS**, not FastGS-Big; 30K runs use MCMC densification, scene-specific budgets and PPISP on real scenes, following original 3DGS image resolutions/splits. Appearance quality is averaged over five runs; reported optimization time is measured on the first RTX 4090 run and excludes subsequent PPISP controller distillation.

Author Table 1 on Mip-NeRF 360 gives SH 192 bytes/G, 6m12s, 28.36 PSNR, .836 SSIM, .222 LPIPS versus neural 28 bytes/G, 4m17s, 28.55, .831, .227. Thus the neural model is faster with higher PSNR but *worse* SSIM and LPIPS. On Tanks & Temples plus Deep Blending, SH 4m10s / 27.96 / .893 / .228 versus neural 2m56s / 28.13 / .893 / .228: two metrics tie. On NeRF Synthetic, SH 1m22s / 34.01 / .971 / .036 versus neural 1m02s / 34.04 / .970 / .038. These comparisons do not establish our strict three-metric target or a result against FastGS-Big/ACE-GS. Its 192→28-byte reduction is per-Gaussian appearance storage; it does not itself measure fewer accepted Gaussian–pixel hits.

The pinned [room configuration](https://github.com/nerficg-project/efficient-gaussian-appearance/blob/bedc372b6e16031feaa5415077a183abb0e326f7/paper_configs/default/neural/room.yaml) lines 33–36 enables `PPISP.CONTROLLER_DISTILLATION` for 5,000 steps, while lines 63–67 explicitly exclude data loading, pretraining and posttraining from the total timing. [Trainer.py](https://github.com/nerficg-project/efficient-gaussian-appearance/blob/bedc372b6e16031feaa5415077a183abb0e326f7/Trainer.py) lines 254–275 executes that post-training distillation loop. Hence the paper's `Train` time is an optimization-interval measurement, **not full process wall time including the enabled distillation**. Room `IMAGE_SCALE_FACTOR: 0.5` (line 146) is a fixed scene input scale; it still needs image-by-image alignment with our pinned effective width before local comparison.

Appendix C.4's five-run Mip-NeRF 360 scene means show neural versus SH improving PSNR for garden, bonsai, counter, kitchen and room, while PSNR falls for bicycle, flowers, stump and treehill. None of the nine Mip-NeRF 360 scenes has strict improvement in all three *rounded mean* metrics. Among the four Tanks & Temples/Deep Blending scenes, only Dr Johnson has a rounded three-metric improvement (PSNR +0.29, SSIM +.001, LPIPS −.002); this is not a significance or cross-scene result. The paper explicitly identifies a moved book in bonsai and trampled grass in garden that appearance models can absorb as view-dependent residuals. These observations do not isolate static reflectance quality from capture inconsistency or establish a low-cost intervention on native FastGS backward.

## Decision

Do not hand Luna a GPU arm based on either direct mechanism or their combination. LFH has a speed–quality tradeoff and changes the per-step image protocol; the compact-appearance paper has a different backbone and mixed quality. Together with the existing RBS, ARF and ACE-GS screens, no candidate yet connects a distinct intervention to an observed representation error, lower dominant native backward work, and an original-loss quality improvement. This is a search-state conclusion, not proof that no such mechanism exists. Next experiment requires a specific, falsifiable causal gate before implementation.

## Polarized opacity prior (additional screen)

Source: [Fast and Compact 3D Gaussian Splatting with Polarized Opacity Prior v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22344v1), HTML SHA256 `9930e5763d8519814a8f4e0ebf2fc5905ad602a392d4fd9883d3189579243db0`, §§3.2–3.3, 4.1–4.2 and Tables 1–2. The paper explicitly connects low-opacity gradient leakage, polarization toward opaque Gaussians, earlier ray termination, fewer Gaussians, and less forward/backward work. To counter the visual artifacts from forcing opacity high, it replaces the primary L1 term with L2 and adds blur-based densification. Thus this **quality-repair plus early-termination** causal story is already prior art; its loss replacement also differs from our fixed original-loss protocol.

The author's gsplat/RTX 4090 Mip-NeRF 360 aggregate reports 279 s, 27.013 PSNR, .760 SSIM, .277 LPIPS versus gsplat 886 s, 27.661, .824, .166, and 3DGS 1304 s, 27.250, .811, .226. It is faster but loses all three image metrics against both in that table. Deep Blending also loses all three against the listed gsplat baseline; Tanks & Temples has slightly higher PSNR (23.618 versus 23.508) but lower SSIM (.811 versus .845) and worse LPIPS (.181 versus .127). FastGS-Big and ACE-GS are not listed. These are author numbers, with no local same-protocol reproduction. They do not justify rerunning opacity polarization under a new name or claiming our triple-quality target.
