# Covariance projection-nullspace update: mathematical and cost gate

Date: 2026-09-28. Analytical/source screen plus one small NumPy linear-algebra check; no model edit, raster render, training or wall-time measurement.

For a fixed 3D Gaussian mean and a rank-two camera projection Jacobian `J1`, choose `n1` in its nullspace. A symmetric covariance edit `ΔΣ=n1 vᵀ+v n1ᵀ` obeys `J1 ΔΣ J1ᵀ=0`; this describes the three-dimensional nullspace of the linear map from symmetric 3×3 covariance to symmetric 2×2 projected covariance. For a second camera's original-loss covariance gradient `G2`, the directional derivative is `2 vᵀ G2 n1`. Thus a second-view constrained descent direction exists when `G2 n1≠0`. This is a change to the 3D covariance, not the earlier isotropic-rotation parameter gauge.

The same algebra reveals the limiting geometry. For two nonparallel view rays `n1,n2`, the common projection-nullspace is generically one-dimensional, spanned by `n1 n2ᵀ+n2 n1ᵀ`; a third general view leaves no nonzero exact common-nullspace edit. A seeded NumPy check of three random orthonormal camera frames (`seed=20260928`) produced covariance-map ranks 3, 5, 6 for one, two and three cameras, with one-view `max|J1ΔΣJ1ᵀ|=2.72e-17`. This is a finite numerical example, not a proof for degenerate camera layouts or finite perspective updates.

**Decision: no current GPU arm.** Preserving projected covariance for camera 1 saves no accepted work in that view. A loss descent on camera 2 need not shrink projection in other views; the special edit `ΔΣ=-2c n1n1ᵀ` does shrink all projected covariances locally but reduces to view-ray thickness flattening, whose original-loss effect is unknown and overlaps surface-style priors. A finite step must keep Σ positive definite and be represented by rotation/log-scales with coherent Adam moments. Per-camera gradients are not free in the existing trainer; opacity compensation/filtering can also prevent exact RGB invariance despite unchanged projected covariance. The proposal lacks a demonstrated multi-view quality and native-work path. 2DGS/GeoSplat and general projected-gradient methods are close prior boundaries; no originality claim is made.
