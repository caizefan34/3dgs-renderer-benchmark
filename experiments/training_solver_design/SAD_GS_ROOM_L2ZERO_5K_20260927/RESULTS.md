The 5K system is 98.274 s faster in complete training process wall than the single timed FastGS-Big run (1.520× FastGS/SAD), with SSIM +.000651 and LPIPS −.010587, but PSNR **−.986500 dB**. It therefore fails the required three-metric noninferiority gate on this one developed scene. The 5K plan also scores −.141947 dB PSNR, +.000330 SSIM and −.003050 LPIPS versus the separate 3K plan. It improves 25/39 PSNR, 28/39 SSIM and 29/39 LPIPS views, but test view `00021.png` (sorted source `DSCF4835.JPG`) loses 9.617912 dB PSNR and dominates that 3K→5K mean. The other 38 PSNR deltas average +.107421 dB. Against FastGS-Big, however, excluding that same view still leaves a descriptive −.734594 dB mean PSNR gap across 38 views, so one fog view is not the whole strong-baseline deficit. **All 39 remain in the official aggregates**; these leave-one-out numbers do not repair the failed gate.

The 5K and 3K runs started from zero separately. At the logged 3K region their Gaussian counts already differ by 10,012 (1,665,316 versus 1,655,304), so their result difference cannot be attributed solely to taking 2K more optimizer steps on an identical 3K model. The cause of trajectory divergence is unproven. A **post-hoc** visual check of `00021.png`, selected because it was the largest negative PSNR delta, shows a broad translucent yellow veil in the 5K render that is absent in the independent 3K render, GT and timed FastGS-Big render; the four copied PNGs are under `qualitative_00021/`. This identifies a concrete failure appearance, not its generating Gaussian(s), a causal edit, or a new remedy. It would be unsound to drop this view or claim longer training generally worsens PSNR.

Stop the duration-only 5K/7K search at this failed gate.

The fog appearance alone is not a quality-and-cost opportunity. An occluding translucent splat might reduce transmittance and shorten the native backward traversal; removing it could *increase* accepted work. Neither the generating primitive(s) nor their training-view recurrence and accepted-hit cost were measured. Source-level prior art already covers ordinary opacity/scale pruning and more faithful ray support. No fog-specific CUDA or training arm is authorized by this observation.

At both the training rasterizer setting `mult=.25` and author `render.py` default `.5`, 5K had lower mean training-image RGB MAE on 7/8 sampled views: `.013158→.012164` at `.25` and `.013512→.012529` at `.5`. The count of accepted Gaussian-pixel pairs in the top residual decile was 10.51%→10.58% and 10.37%→10.44%, respectively, close to the selected pixels' 10% area. Thus the test outlier is not reproduced as a broad sampled training-view residual regression or a disproportionate high-residual forward-work hotspot.

The counter includes only pairs passing the rasterizer power/alpha (`α≥1/255`) and early-transmittance (`test_T≥1e-4`) checks; it is not backward FLOPs or elapsed cost. Top-1% projected-footprint Gaussians account for 13.90%→13.88% of accepted work at `mult=.25` (18.60%→18.45% at `.5`); front-visible Gaussians with filtered opacity 0.1–0.9 account for 60.18%→54.81% (`.25`) and 59.05%→53.55% (`.5`). Output pixels with opacity 0.1–0.9 account for only 1.02%→1.10% (`.25`) and 1.05%→0.91% (`.5`) of accepted work.
