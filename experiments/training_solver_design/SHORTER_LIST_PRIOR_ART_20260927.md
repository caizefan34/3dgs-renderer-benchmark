# Shorter Gaussian lists: bounded prior-art screen

Checked 2026-09-27 against [Speeding Up the Learning of 3D Gaussians with Much Shorter Gaussian Lists v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09277v1), HTML SHA256 `30345452e5e642df64892480ea8439355cd5fe6dad19e03743f62487572d2bef`. The [author code](https://github.com/MachinePerceptionLab/ShorterSplatting) was discovered at `main@0bac93473142d1fef4acef721f9dee8ac383d4a4`; no local reproduction was run.

The paper's §§4.1–4.2 explicitly target long pixel Gaussian lists, which drive both forward memory access and backward work. It periodically resets *all* Gaussian scales downward and adds an alpha-compositing weight-entropy penalty so that each Gaussian concentrates its effect on fewer pixels. It combines these with DashGaussian's coarse-to-fine resolution schedule (§5.2). The implemented objective is therefore not our unchanged original L1+D-SSIM loss, and early training is not at full effective resolution. The backbone is LiteGS stable with 150 epochs of 200 sampled images, 30K iterations. Experiments use a single RTX 5090 D, not our A100.

The author Table 3 directly compares its 0.4M-Gaussian setting with **FastGS default**, not FastGS-Big, on Mip-NeRF 360: FastGS 88.55 s / 27.43 PSNR / .797 SSIM / .261 LPIPS; theirs 66.32 s / 26.50 / .754 / .314. This is faster but worse in all three quality metrics. Table 1's 3.3M LiteGS-based Mip-NeRF 360 average likewise moves 191.17 s / 27.75 / .822 / .208 to 99.58 s / 27.28 / .810 / .224; the full combination has a quality tradeoff. Its Table 4 ablations show scale reset and entropy independently reduce time while lowering at least PSNR/SSIM against LiteGS. These are author-reported results, not same-protocol local measurements.

**Boundary:** "shrink footprints / sharpen alpha weights to shorten lists and save backward work" is already an explicit published mechanism. Simply replacing their scheduler or loss with our protocol does not make that core original or prove the three-metric goal. A different candidate would need an observed representation error, an intervention distinct from global scale reset/entropy/opacity polarization, and evidence that its quality repair also reduces native accepted-work or complete training time. Do not launch a renamed scale-reset/entropy arm with Luna.
