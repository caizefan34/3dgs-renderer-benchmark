# EPIC-05 HiGS P99 longest-first tile scheduling

Benchmark commit: `8d797a3b10158dab29482d8119c4c6cb204f594e`  
gsplat source: `77ab983ffe43420b2131669cb35776b883ca4c3c` plus

Host: EPIC-05, one A100-SXM4-80GB cohort, five canonical 1080p scenes.

## Mechanism

The existing HiGS raster kernel computes a Gaussian mask for every active fine
tile and dynamically assigns tiles to warps. The prototype counts set bits in
those masks, performs a 32-lane bitonic sort, and places the tiles with the most
Gaussian work first. This is longest-processing-time-first scheduling intended
to reduce the final straggler warp and improve P99 latency.

Mean FPS is 719.43 versus 724.01 for canonical fixed tile-8 HiGS, an aggregate
**-0.63%**. P99 improves on Truck, is unchanged on Garden, and regresses on
Train, Bicycle, and Bonsai. All five scenes preserve the fixed renderer's
PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS to numerical noise, and peak VRAM is unchanged.

## Decision

Always sorting every macro batch is not a general acceleration. Truck shows
that longest-first scheduling can help when tile work is sufficiently
imbalanced, but the bitonic-sort overhead dominates the other canonical
scenes. Idea 12 is therefore marked **measured, no aggregate gain**.

The evidence suggests a narrower follow-up: compute a cheap warp-level
dispersion signal and retain the original queue unless `max/mean` or variance
indicates enough imbalance to repay the sort. This conditional policy must be
measured independently; Truck's gain is not generalized as an expected result.
